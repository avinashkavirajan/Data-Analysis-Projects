A sales dashboard, and the data problem it uncovered

A hardware company's sales team kept three years of transactions in a MySQL database but had no quick way to see performance. I built a Power BI dashboard to answer their everyday questions, and found that almost half of the revenue couldn't be traced back to a product.

The problem

The sales data covered 38 customers, 14 Indian cities and about 340 product codes. Simple questions such as "who are our biggest customers?", "which markets drive revenue?" or "is revenue going up?" meant writing a new database query every time.

The goal was one dashboard a sales manager could open, filter by year or month, and read in a minute.

What I built

The data moves from the company's database to the dashboard in four steps.

  1. Extract

    Loaded five MySQL tables: transactions, customers, products, markets and dates.

  2. Clean

    Prepared the data in Power Query before modelling.

  3. Model

    Linked each sale to its customer, product, market and date in a data model.

  4. Visualise

    Revenue, quantity, trend, markets, and the top 5 customers and products, filterable by year and month.

Data quality review

A dashboard is only as good as the data behind it. Checking the source tables directly showed several issues a business user would never see on the charts.

IssueSizeRecommendation
Product codes used in sales but missing from the product master60 codes · 55,159 rowsAdd the missing products at the source; until then, label "(Blank)" clearly on the dashboard.
Sales with a zero or negative amount1,611 rowsConfirm whether these are returns, free samples or errors, and filter or flag them.
Currency values with hidden characters ("INR" and "INR" plus a line break), and 4 rows in USDAll 150,283 rowsStandardise the currency field and convert or remove USD rows before summing revenue.
The same market listed twice under two codes (Bhopal)2 codesMerge to one market code so totals aren't split.
Markets with no sales (New York, Paris) and no zone2 marketsRemove or tag as test data so they don't appear in filters.

This is the part of the work I'd take into a real engagement: the charts answer the business's questions, and the data review tells them which answers they can rely on.
